Kepaniwai Heritage Gardens, a free county park at the base of Iao Valley, commemorates Maui's multicultural plantation history through seven distinct cultural pavilions — Chinese, English, Filipino, Hawaiian, Japanese, Korean, and Portuguese — each reflecting the architectural style and garden traditions of the immigrant communities that shaped the island's workforce.

The park experienced real disruption in 2026: back-to-back Kona low storms in March caused severe erosion and flooding along the Wailuku River, undermining Picnic Pavilions 1 and 2 and downing at least one large tree. The park closed for repairs and reopened in June 2026, though the two damaged picnic pavilions remain closed while repair work continues — visitors should follow posted warning signage and exercise some caution around those specific areas.

The seven cultural garden pavilions themselves were unaffected by the storm damage and remain fully open and free to explore, making this a worthwhile, no-cost stop for families interested in Maui's immigrant and plantation history, easily combined with a nearby Iao Valley visit on the same outing.
