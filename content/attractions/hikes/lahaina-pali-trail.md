The Lahaina Pali Trail follows the historic route that connected West Maui to the central valley before the coastal highway existed, climbing over the dry southern flank of the West Maui Mountains between Ma'alaea and Ukumehame. It's a genuinely demanding hike — 5.5 miles one-way with about 1,587 feet of elevation gain, rated difficult, and typically taking around four hours in each direction.

Two trailheads bookend the route. The western (Ukumehame) trailhead sits about three miles from Ma'alaea Harbor, just past the Lahaina Pali Tunnel, with parking for 15-20 cars in a shaded pullout. The eastern trailhead, near the Highway 30/380 junction on the Kihei side, sits on private land with no official parking facility — two-wheel-drive cars should park along the highway rather than attempt the rough access road.

Because it's a point-to-point trail rather than a loop, most hikers don't attempt the full one-way crossing without arranging a shuttle or second car. The more common approach is an out-and-back from the Ukumehame side up to the ridge of wind turbines, turning around once the views and workout feel like enough. Either way, come prepared for heat, wind, loose rock, and almost no shade along the entire route.
