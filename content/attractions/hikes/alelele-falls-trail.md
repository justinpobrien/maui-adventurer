Alelele Falls is a short, little-visited detour past the main Road to Hana crowds — located beyond Haleakala National Park's Kipahulu district and past mile marker 39, near a distinctive white bridge over Alelele Stream. The hike itself is brief, around 0.4 miles and 15 to 30 minutes each way, following the stream upstream to the base of a modest, seasonal waterfall.

Flow here depends heavily on recent rainfall — after dry stretches the falls can thin out considerably, while a good rain upstream makes for a fuller cascade. The trail is not officially maintained, meaning tall grass, uneven ground, and the occasional need to wade are all part of the experience, along with a real rockfall risk on the overhead terrain following rain or high winds.

This trail is currently closed indefinitely due to severe erosion and landslide risk, which supersedes older write-ups describing it as an easy, lightly trafficked detour. The closure is a safety measure rather than a technicality, and it should be respected rather than worked around using outdated directions.
