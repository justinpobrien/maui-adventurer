Molokini Crater is Maui's signature snorkel excursion — a partially submerged volcanic crater rising from the channel between Maui and Kaho'olawe, accessible only by boat and protected as a Marine Life Conservation District. The payoff for the boat ride is exceptional water clarity, with visibility commonly cited at 150 feet and reportedly up to 200 feet on the clearest early-morning trips.

The crater hosts more than 250 reef fish species and 38 types of hard coral, with regular sightings of moray eels, octopus, reef sharks, and rays alongside the usual reef fish. Because it's a protected conservation district, there's no fishing and no anchoring on coral, which has helped keep the underwater environment in notably good condition compared to more heavily trafficked shore spots.

Roughly two dozen operators run trips out to Molokini, with pricing that varies significantly — afternoon tours run $95 to $140 per person, while premium early-morning options (generally offering the best visibility and smallest crowds) can run $150 to $300 or more. Since there's no shore access, choosing a tour is a mandatory part of planning a Molokini visit rather than an optional upgrade.
