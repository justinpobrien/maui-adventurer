Sliding Sands, known in Hawaiian as Keonehe'ehe'e, is the main route down into Haleakala's otherworldly volcanic crater — a landscape of cinder cones, reddish-brown volcanic soil, and near-total silence that feels closer to Mars than to the tropical Maui most visitors expect. The full trail runs about 11 miles, dropping roughly 2,800 feet in just the first 4 miles down to the crater floor, and it's rated difficult, recommended only for experienced, well-prepared hikers.

The name is a fair warning: the descent is genuinely fast and easy on loose volcanic sand, which makes the return climb — at elevations around 7,000 to 10,000 feet, in full sun with essentially no shade — the real test of the hike. Many visitors do a shorter out-and-back rather than the full traverse, turning around once the return climb starts to feel like enough.

Access runs through the standard Haleakala National Park entrance fee, with no separate permit required for daytime hiking. The one added wrinkle is timing: the park restricts entry between 3am and 7am, and reserving a sunrise viewing slot through the summit's permit lottery is required during that window even for hikers who have no interest in the sunrise crowd itself — worth avoiding by simply starting after 7am.
