Papalaua Wayside Park is a long, narrow strip of shoreline squeezed between Honoapi'ilani Highway and the ocean, better known among locals as a low-key camping spot than a snorkeling destination. Shade from kiawe trees runs along much of the park, and the beach itself offers calm, easy swimming without the crowds of the resort beaches further north.

Facilities are basic by design: picnic tables, barbecue grills, and portable toilets, but no running water, so anyone planning to spend real time here — especially overnight — needs to bring their own supply. The ground underfoot has kiawe thorns scattered through it, making closed-toe shoes a genuinely practical call rather than overcaution.

Camping is the park's main claim to fame, and it requires a permit from Maui County obtained by walk-in application at the county office (call ahead to check availability). Tent camping is permitted nightly except Tuesday and Wednesday, with a four-night maximum; non-resident fees run roughly $50/adult and $6/minor per night midweek, and $100/adult and $12/minor on weekends. Because the park runs right alongside a busy highway, don't expect the same seclusion as more remote camping spots on Maui.
