Kaupo Gap is the dramatic breach in Haleakala's crater rim where the volcano's southeast wall gives way, letting a trail descend more than 6,100 feet over 8.4 miles from the high-elevation crater floor down to the remote, dry Kaupo coast — one of the starkest elevation and climate transitions found anywhere on Maui.

As a viewpoint rather than a hike, Kaupo Gap is best taken in either from crater-rim overlooks near Haleakala's summit, where the gap opens up as a striking notch in the otherwise continuous rim, or from the remote Kaupo coast road on the island's far southeast side, looking back up toward the volcano. The full trail through the gap crosses private ranch land in its lower stretches and requires a car shuttle or pre-arranged pickup, making it a serious backcountry undertaking rather than a casual stop.

For most visitors, this is a scenic feature to admire from a distance — either glimpsed from the crater rim or from the isolated southeast coastline — rather than a destination to walk into on a typical day trip.
