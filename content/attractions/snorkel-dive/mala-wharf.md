Mala Wharf is a long-standing Lahaina dive and snorkel site built around the remains of a historic pier that partially collapsed decades ago, leaving submerged concrete pilings and structure that have since become an informal artificial reef. The site has drawn local divers for years, valued for easy shore access relative to boat-only sites nearby.

The wharf's status became a genuine question after the 2023 Lahaina wildfire, but it reopened to recreational boaters, swimmers, and divers in October 2023 following repair work. That said, Hawaii's Division of Boating and Ocean Recreation has flagged environmental concerns tied to the fire — burned structures containing materials like asbestos and metal raised risks of water contamination and potential coral disease, and monitoring of those impacts has continued since reopening.

Given both the informal nature of the site (swimming and diving here is explicitly at your own risk, with no lifeguard) and the fire-related environmental questions, this is a spot worth checking current conditions on before visiting, ideally through a local dive shop or the state's boating division rather than relying on older, pre-2023 write-ups.
