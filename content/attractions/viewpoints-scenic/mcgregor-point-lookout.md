McGregor Point Lookout is an elevated roadside pullout along Honoapiilani Highway between Ma'alaea and Lahaina, widely considered one of Maui's most reliable land-based whale-watching spots thanks to its clear, panoramic view over the 'Au'au Channel — a stretch of water where migrating humpback whales concentrate each winter.

Whale season runs from December through mid-April, with peak numbers typically mid-January through March, when thousands of North Pacific humpback whales complete their 3,000-plus mile migration from Alaska to Hawaii's warm coastal waters. During this window, Pacific Whale Foundation naturalists staff the lookout daily from 8am to 2pm, offering free educational information, spotting tips, and answering visitor questions — a nice added layer versus a purely self-guided stop.

Given the lookout's popularity and modest parking area, arriving early in the day gives the best chance at a spot, and bringing binoculars significantly improves the experience since whale breaches and tail slaps can happen at a fair distance offshore.
