Pu'u Keka'a — better known to visitors as Black Rock — is the volcanic promontory that splits Ka'anapali Beach in two, and it holds real weight in Hawaiian tradition: it was believed to be a leaping point where souls departed for the spirit world, and it was a favorite cliff-jumping spot of Maui's last ruling chief, Kahekili. Since 1963, the Sheraton Maui has honored that history with a nightly cliff-diving ceremony at sunset, complete with torch-lighting, a chant, and a conch shell call before a diver leaps from the rock.

By day, the water around the base of the rock is one of the easiest, most reliable snorkeling spots on the island. The formation creates a natural reef edge close to shore, and it's common to spot green sea turtles gliding through alongside parrotfish, needlefish, and the occasional eagle ray — all without needing a boat or a long swim.

Access is via the beach between the Sheraton Maui and the Ka'anapali Beach Hotel, and it's free and open to the public despite sitting on resort grounds. Confident swimmers sometimes cliff-jump here outside the official ceremony, but the rocks and currents demand real caution and local knowledge — this isn't a spot to improvise a jump.
