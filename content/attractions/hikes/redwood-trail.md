The Redwood Trail is the signature route through Polipoli Spring State Recreation Area, descending from the main trailhead at about 6,200 feet through a genuinely unlikely sight for a Hawaiian island: a planted grove of towering redwood trees, alongside other conifers introduced during early-20th-century reforestation efforts. At roughly 1.7 miles with about 900 feet of elevation gain, it's rated moderate, dropping to an old ranger cabin site around 5,300 feet before connecting to the Plum and Boundary Trail junctions.

There's a real access question worth flagging here: while Hawaii's Division of State Parks still lists the trail on its official site, some current trail-tracking platforms show it marked as closed. That discrepancy suggests either a temporary closure, incomplete data, or a partial-access situation — any of which is worth confirming directly with DLNR or a local hiking guide before building a trip specifically around this trail.

Getting to the trailhead requires the same rough, largely unpaved final stretch of Waipoli Road as the rest of the Polipoli trail network, with 4WD effectively required. The payoff, if accessible, is a genuinely unusual forest experience — cool, quiet, and visually unlike anywhere else on Maui.
