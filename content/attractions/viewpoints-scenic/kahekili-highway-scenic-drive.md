Kahekili Highway (Route 340) is Maui's wilder, far less-traveled alternative to the Road to Hana — a narrow, twisting road along the island's remote northern coastline connecting Kapalua to Wailuku the long way, around the back of the West Maui Mountains. It's a genuinely different kind of scenic drive: rugged cliffs, crashing surf, and long stretches without another car in sight.

The road itself demands respect: pavement frequently narrows to a single lane shared by traffic in both directions, with steep drop-offs on one side and bare rock walls on the other, and few pullouts or guardrails anywhere along the route. It's also prone to rockslides and closures, particularly after heavy rain — as recently as March 2026, storm damage forced a closure northbound at Waihe'e School with local traffic only permitted, underscoring how quickly conditions here can change.

Given the challenges, this drive isn't for every visitor — nervous drivers, large rental vehicles, or anyone driving after dark or in poor visibility should think twice or choose an alternate route. For confident drivers looking for a quieter, more adventurous version of Maui's famous coastal drives, though, it delivers genuinely spectacular and uncrowded scenery.
