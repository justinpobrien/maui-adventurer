Polipoli Spring State Recreation Area sits at around 6,200 feet on Haleakala's upper slopes and feels almost nothing like the rest of Maui — a cool, often fog-shrouded forest of redwood, sugi, cypress, ash, and plum trees planted during a reforestation effort nearly a century ago. Roughly 10 miles of interconnected trails wind through the area, with the classic combination — linking the Redwood, Plum, Haleakala Ridge, and Polipoli Trails into a loop — running approximately 3.5 to 4.7 miles depending on the exact combination hiked.

Getting there is arguably the hardest part of the whole outing. Waipoli Road, the access route, turns unpaved and genuinely rough for its final few miles, with posted signage requiring 4WD and reports of fines issued to 2WD vehicles found past that point. Without a 4WD vehicle, visitors are looking at roughly 4 additional miles of walking on rutted, often muddy road just to reach the main trailhead — a serious addition to an already moderate day.

Once there, the hiking itself is moderate rather than extreme, winding through cool forest that feels dramatically different from Maui's tropical lowlands. Plum trees along the Plum Trail bear fruit in season, and the elevation brings noticeably colder temperatures than almost anywhere else on the island, worth dressing for.
