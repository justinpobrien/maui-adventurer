Ka'anapali is the beach that put West Maui on the map — three miles of golden sand stretching past a wall of resort hotels, with a paved beachwalk connecting the whole stretch. It's less a single beach than a corridor: swimmers, paddleboarders, and sunset-watchers all share the same shoreline that once served as a royal playground for Maui's ali'i.

The swimming here is easy and forgiving for most of the year, with the calmest water typically found toward the center of the beach near Whalers Village. Snorkelers should head to the northern end, where the beach meets Pu'u Keka'a (Black Rock) for the best fish-watching close to shore. Facilities are spread along the beachwalk rather than concentrated in one spot, so plan around whichever hotel or public access point you're nearest to.

Parking is the main friction point — public lots are small and fill early, so arriving before 9am or parking at Hanaka'o'o Beach Park and walking in are the most reliable options. Evenings bring a different draw: this is one of Maui's best perches for watching the sun drop behind Lana'i, especially paired with a walk down to Black Rock for the nightly cliff-diving ceremony.
