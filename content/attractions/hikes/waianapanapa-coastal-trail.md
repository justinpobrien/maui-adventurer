The coastal trail through Waianapanapa State Park traces a rugged shoreline of barren lava rock, offering some of the most dramatic ocean views along the entire Hana coast. Sea arches, blowholes, and small offshore islets punctuate the route, with stands of native hala (screwpine) trees providing occasional shade and framing views back toward Haleakala's lower slopes.

The trail effectively runs in two directions from the park: a northern section of roughly 2.3 miles round trip and a southern leg — officially the Ke Ala Loa O Maui/Piilani Trail — of about 2.2 miles round trip. Both are rated moderately challenging, with uneven, exposed lava terrain rather than technical climbing, and each takes under an hour for a typical hiker.

Because this trail sits inside Waianapanapa State Park, it requires the same advance reservation as visiting the black sand beach — $10 per vehicle plus $5 per person for non-residents, booked online ahead of time, with no same-day or walk-in access. Sturdy shoes are worth it here; the lava surface is sharp and uneven in stretches, and there's little shade for most of the route.
