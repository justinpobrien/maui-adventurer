Kahakuloa Head — known in Hawaiian as Pu'u Koa'e — is a striking 636-foot volcanic promontory towering over the tiny, remote village of Kahakuloa on Maui's rugged northwest coast. It's one of the most photographed landmarks along the Kahekili Highway loop, and it also comes with a clear, current safety warning worth taking seriously.

While informal trail data lists a short loop (about 1.2 miles, 180 feet of elevation gain) as technically "easy," that figure describes distance and grade only — it doesn't capture the actual hazard. Multiple current sources are explicit that the narrow, steep cliff trail up the headland is dangerous and hiking it is not recommended. This is a landmark to view and photograph from safe, legal roadside pullouts rather than a route to attempt on foot.

Getting to the area at all requires driving Kahekili Highway, a famously narrow and winding stretch of road that many rental car companies restrict or exclude from coverage — worth checking your rental agreement before setting out, regardless of whether you plan to hike.
