Puohokamoa Falls illustrates how quickly Maui waterfall access can change: what older trip reports describe as a swimmable, publicly reachable spot no longer applies here. The upper falls now sit on private property and have been fenced off entirely, with previously used trails closed or restricted — attempting to reach them by following outdated directions risks trespassing on posted land.

The lower falls remain viewable, though only through a paid attraction: the Garden of Eden Arboretum, located a half mile past mile marker 10, charges admission but grants access to a well-maintained garden with a clear view of the 200-foot lower falls from a safe distance. This is a look-don't-touch experience rather than a swimming destination.

For anyone hoping to check off "Puohokamoa Falls" on a waterfall-chasing Road to Hana itinerary, the Garden of Eden is now effectively the only legitimate way to see it — worth building the entry fee into the day's budget rather than searching for a free roadside pull-off that no longer offers real access.
