The Kaupo Trail is Haleakala's most demanding day (or overnight) hike — a long, punishing descent that starts near the crater floor inside Haleakala National Park and drops through the rugged Kaupo Gap all the way to sea level near Kaupo village on Maui's remote southeast coast. Total distance runs roughly 8 to 11 miles one-way depending on the exact exit point, with an elevation change reported around 6,000+ feet.

Difficulty here isn't hyperbole. Large stretches offer no shade, trail signage is notoriously easy to lose, and once hikers cross out of the national park boundary into the Kaupo Gap itself, the trail is no longer maintained by the NPS at all — it crosses private land via a hiking easement, and the route can be brushy, faint, and genuinely hard to follow. This is a route for well-prepared, experienced hikers only, ideally in a small group with a way to get back to a vehicle at the opposite end, since it's a one-way point-to-point hike rather than a loop.

The upper trailhead falls within Haleakala National Park's $30 per vehicle entrance fee (valid 3 days); there's no separate fee at the Kaupo end. Overnight backcountry camping within the park section requires a permit, though the lower Kaupo Gap portion isn't governed by NPS permitting since it's outside park boundaries.
