The Keanae Peninsula Lookout is one of the classic quick stops on the Road to Hana, offering a bird's-eye view over the Keanae Peninsula's patchwork of taro (kalo) fields, worked much as they have been for generations, with the small village and a rugged lava-rock coastline stretching out below.

Finding it takes a little attentiveness: there's no official signpost marking the pull-off, so the landmark to watch for is a yellow tsunami warning speaker near mile marker 17, on the ocean side of the highway. The stop itself is brief — most visitors spend 10 to 15 minutes taking photos and enjoying the view before continuing on.

The ocean here is genuinely rough, with powerful waves breaking against jagged lava rock, so this is strictly a scenic viewpoint rather than a beach stop. Roughly at the halfway mark between Kahului and Hana, it's a natural point to stretch legs partway through the long drive.
