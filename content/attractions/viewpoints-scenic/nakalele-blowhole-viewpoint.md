Nakalele Blowhole, near Nakalele Point on West Maui's rugged north shore along Kahekili Highway, is one of the island's most dramatic natural features: ocean swells forced through a lava tube erupt in a powerful vertical spray, sometimes reaching impressive heights when conditions align.

It's also one of Maui's genuinely dangerous attractions, and this needs to be stated plainly rather than softened: there are no barriers or warning signage keeping visitors back, and the same suction that creates the dramatic spray has pulled people into the ocean when standing too close, with injuries and deaths reported over the years. Slippery wet lava rock and unpredictable rogue waves compound the risk.

Small dirt parking areas near mile markers 38 and 38.5 provide access, though space is limited and the road narrows in places. Visitors who do go should stay at least 100 feet from the blowhole itself, wear non-slip shoes, never turn their back on the ocean, and check conditions beforehand — this is a look-from-a-safe-distance attraction, not a place to pose for photos up close.
