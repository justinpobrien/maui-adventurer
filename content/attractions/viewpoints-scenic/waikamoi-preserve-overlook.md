Waikamoi Preserve, managed by The Nature Conservancy, protects a rare stretch of native Hawaiian cloud forest on Haleakala's slopes — critical habitat for several critically endangered bird species found nowhere else on Earth, including the kiwikiu (Maui parrotbill) and 'akohekohe. Because it's private conservation land, access is intentionally limited rather than open to casual hiking.

The main way to experience the preserve directly is through The Nature Conservancy's monthly guided hike, held the second Saturday of each month and capped at just 12 participants — sign-ups open exactly 60 days in advance at 8am Hawai'i time and fill almost immediately. For visitors who don't manage to snag a spot, roadside pull-offs along the Haleakala Highway on the drive up to the summit offer glimpses of the preserve's forested slopes from a respectful distance.

It's worth distinguishing this from the separate, publicly accessible Waikamoi Ridge Trail near mile marker 9.5 on the Hana Highway — a standalone 1.1-mile hike that, while bearing the same name, is a different (and much more accessible) experience from the protected preserve itself.
