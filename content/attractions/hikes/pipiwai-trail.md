The Pipiwai Trail, part of Haleakala National Park's Kipahulu District, is one of Maui's most rewarding day hikes and arguably its most photographed — a 4-mile round trip that climbs through a giant banyan tree grove, past cascading pools, and into a genuinely otherworldly bamboo forest before reaching the base of Waimoku Falls, a 400-foot cascade tumbling down a fern-draped cliff face.

The hike is rated moderate, gaining about 800 feet over its length and typically taking up to three hours round trip. Early sections pass Makahiku Falls overlook before the trail climbs through increasingly dense bamboo, crossing a boardwalk built specifically to protect the fragile understory from foot traffic. The payoff at the end — Waimoku Falls — is worth the buildup, especially after rain when the flow is at its fullest.

Access runs through the standard Haleakala National Park entrance fee of $30 per vehicle, valid for three days and covering both this district and the summit area (cashless payment only). No separate trail permit is required, though anyone wanting to catch sunrise at the summit needs a $1 reservation for early entry between 3 and 7am — irrelevant for a Kipahulu-side hike, but worth knowing if pairing this with a Haleakala sunrise trip.
