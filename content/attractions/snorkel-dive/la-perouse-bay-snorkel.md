La Perouse Bay marks the literal end of the paved road in south Maui, and its snorkeling reflects that remoteness — genuinely good, but demanding more effort and experience than the resort-adjacent beaches further north. The bay itself often has disappointingly poor visibility for snorkeling, but conditions improve significantly toward the northern end, near the boundary with the Ahihi-Kinau Natural Area Reserve, where clearer water and better marine life make the extra swim worthwhile.

The reef here has an unusual profile: coral heads often don't reach the surface, letting snorkelers glide above a colorful underwater landscape rather than navigating around shallow coral fingers. Large schools of fish and the occasional pod of spinner dolphins are among the draws for those willing to deal with the site's challenges.

Those challenges are real: a difficult entry point, remote location far from any lifeguard or quick help, and winds that pick up fast and roughen the water, especially by midday. This combination makes La Perouse Bay a spot best reserved for confident, experienced snorkelers rather than a family outing — mornings offer the calmest conditions before trade winds take over.
