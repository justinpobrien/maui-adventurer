Paia began as a plantation town built around the Paia Sugar Mill, which operated from the 1880s until 2000 and employed a genuinely multicultural workforce of Japanese, Chinese, Portuguese, Filipino, and Korean laborers. When the plantation economy declined, the town found a new identity: hippie culture arrived in the 1970s, followed by a booming surf and windsurfing scene in the 1980s that put Paia — specifically nearby Ho'okipa Beach — on the map as a world windsurfing capital.

That layered history shows up in the town's current character: a genuinely eclectic mix of art galleries (Maui Crafts Guild, Maui Hands), boutiques (Biasa Rose, Paia Trading Company, Aloha Boutique), and surf shops (Da Kine, Honolua Surf Co, Hi-Tech) packed into a compact, walkable main street.

Beyond shopping, Paia functions as the practical gateway to the Road to Hana — the last real town before the long, winding drive begins — making it a natural stop for gas, coffee, and last-minute supplies alongside its own worth as a browsing destination.
