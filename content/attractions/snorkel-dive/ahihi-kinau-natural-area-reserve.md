Ahihi-Kinau Natural Area Reserve protects 1,238 acres of marine ecosystem and rare anchialine ponds along Maui's southern coast, making it one of the more ecologically significant snorkel spots on the island rather than just another pretty cove. Snorkeling is permitted in the main accessible section, though fishing is banned entirely and the reserve's anchialine pools are off-limits to all visitors to protect their fragile, specialized wildlife.

Conditions here are notably weather-dependent. Summer brings south swells that can churn up the water and make snorkeling genuinely hazardous, while winter tends to deliver calmer, clearer days. Regardless of season, mornings before the trade winds pick up offer the best visibility and safest conditions — afternoons here can turn choppy fast.

Given its reserve status, visitors are expected to tread lightly: no touching coral or marine life, no collecting, and general respect for what's a genuinely fragile coastal ecosystem rather than a standard beach park. Access to the main area runs 5:30am to 7:30pm daily, with no entry fee.
