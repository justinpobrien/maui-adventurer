Big Beach — Oneloa in Hawaiian, meaning "long sand" — is Maui's largest undeveloped beach, a broad, steep stretch of golden sand backed by kiawe trees and anchored at its northern end by the cinder cone Pu'u Ola'i. It's a genuinely striking beach, and one that demands real respect: this is one of Maui's most dangerous stretches of sand for swimmers.

The hazard is the shorebreak. Waves here break directly onto steeply sloped, hard-packed sand, and when surf is up — common in spring and summer, and during southerly Kona storms — the impact has caused dislocated joints, broken necks, and spinal injuries. Strong rip currents compound the risk. This isn't a beach for children, inexperienced swimmers, or anyone unwilling to read conditions before going in past their knees.

Facilities are basic: portable toilets, picnic tables, and a lifeguard station within Makena State Park. As of recent years, the park charges non-residents $5 per person for entry plus $10 per vehicle for parking (free for Hawaii residents with valid state ID), across three lots that fill by mid-morning. Despite the risk, Big Beach remains one of Maui's best sunset spots, and a short bluff-top trail from its northern end leads to the more secluded Little Beach beyond.
