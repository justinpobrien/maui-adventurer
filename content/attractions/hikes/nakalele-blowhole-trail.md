The walk out to Nakalele Blowhole is short, but it leads to one of the more genuinely hazardous natural features on Maui's tourist trail. The trail itself covers roughly a mile round trip over rocky, uneven lava terrain, typically taking 15 to 25 minutes each way depending on photo stops and footing — an easy distance, but not an easy surface.

The blowhole itself forces seawater through a lava tube with enough force to send spray dozens of feet into the air, and that force is the danger: people have died or been seriously injured here after being knocked off their feet by the spray or swept off the surrounding lava shelf by unexpected wave surges. There are no barriers or posted warnings at the site itself, so the standard safety advice — staying at least 100 feet back at all times — depends entirely on visitors exercising their own judgment.

Parking near the trailhead, around mile markers 38 to 38.5 on Kahekili Highway, is now paid, with the fee covering a zone that includes both the blowhole area and D.T. Fleming State Park Beach. Spaces are limited and fill early, so this works best as a stop earlier in a West Maui driving day rather than an afternoon add-on.
