The St. Anthony Wreck is Maui's most popular artificial reef dive — a 65-foot shrimp boat intentionally sunk in October 1997 off Keawakapu Beach, now encrusted with coral growth and serving as a reliable hangout for green sea turtles. Resting in about 70 feet of water, with the top of the wreck around 55 feet, this is squarely a certified-diver destination rather than a snorkeling spot.

The wreck sits near a companion cement-and-tire artificial reef structure a few hundred yards offshore, and together the two features create a small but rich dive site that draws repeat visits from local dive operations. Several Kihei-based charter companies, including Mike Severns Diving, run trips to St. Anthony, often pairing it as a second dive alongside a nearby reef site.

Reaching it from shore is technically possible — roughly a 0.4-mile surface swim on a 240-degree heading from the base of the stairs at Keawakapu, sometimes covered by scooter or kayak — but most divers opt for a boat charter given the distance and depth involved. This isn't a site for inexperienced divers or anyone without proper certification.
