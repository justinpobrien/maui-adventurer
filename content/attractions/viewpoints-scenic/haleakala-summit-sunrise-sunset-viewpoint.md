Watching sunrise or sunset from Haleakala's 10,000-foot summit is one of Maui's defining experiences: standing above the cloud layer as the sky changes color over the vast, otherworldly crater below. The two options — sunrise and sunset — differ meaningfully in logistics, even though the visual payoff is comparable.

Sunrise requires a separate reservation beyond the standard park entrance fee: a $1 vehicle reservation booked through Recreation.gov, with tickets releasing 60 days in advance at 7am Hawai'i time and a smaller batch opening 48 hours before each date. Any non-commercial vehicle in the four highest-elevation parking areas (Summit, Haleakala Visitor Center, Kalahaku, and Leleiwi) needs this reservation between 3 and 7am. Missing out on a reservation doesn't lock you out entirely — the summit is open without one after 7am — but it does mean missing the actual sunrise moment.

Sunset, by contrast, requires no advance reservation at all, and draws roughly a third the crowd of sunrise while offering an equally striking above-the-clouds experience. Either way, the standard $30 park vehicle entrance fee (valid 3 days, shared with the Kipahulu/Ohe'o Gulch district) applies, and summit temperatures can drop close to freezing regardless of the warm weather down at sea level — warm layers are essential, not optional.
