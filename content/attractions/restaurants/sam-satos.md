Sam Sato's is a genuine Wailuku institution, best known for a dish found almost nowhere else on Maui in quite the same form: dry noodles, or dry mein — wavy saimin noodles tossed in a proprietary seasoning with char siu pork and bean sprouts, served with a side of dashi broth for dipping rather than swimming in soup. It's the kind of local dish that draws both longtime residents and food-curious visitors specifically to try it.

Beyond the signature noodles, the menu covers breakfast standards (eggs, omelettes, hash browns, breakfast meats) and a range of noodle bowls, soups, and plate lunches with rice, along with hamburgers and sandwiches for anyone wanting something more familiar. It's a straightforward, unpretentious local diner rather than a scenic destination restaurant.

Hours are limited compared to most Maui restaurants — open Tuesday through Saturday, 7am to 2pm, with manju (a sweet filled pastry) available for pickup until 4pm, and closed entirely on Sunday and Monday.
