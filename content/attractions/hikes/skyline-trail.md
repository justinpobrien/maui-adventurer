The Skyline Trail is Haleakala's longest and most dramatic elevation traverse, descending from the 10,023-foot summit area at Pu'u 'Ula'ula (Red Hill) down the volcano's southwest flank toward Waipoli Road near Polipoli — a drop of roughly 2,600 to 3,000 feet over about 6.8 to 7 miles. It's rated difficult and typically approached as a long day hike given the elevation change, remoteness, and the fact that most of the demanding climbing happens on the return trip if hiked as an out-and-back.

This is a trail with real access uncertainty worth flagging directly: some current trail-tracking sources list it as closed, while other recent write-ups describe it as an active, if strenuous, route. Sources also disagree on whether a separate permit is required to access the summit-side trailhead beyond the standard park entrance fee. Given the remoteness and the conflicting information, this is one to confirm directly with Haleakala National Park rangers or a local hiking guide before committing time to it.

If open and accessible, the trail's appeal is its sheer range — starting in barren, high-altitude cinder terrain near the summit and ending in green upcountry shrubland thousands of feet lower, a dramatic ecological transition compressed into a single hike.
