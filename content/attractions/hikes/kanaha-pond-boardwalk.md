Kanaha Pond State Wildlife Sanctuary sits in an unlikely spot — sandwiched between downtown Kahului and the airport — but it's one of only two major wetland habitats left on Maui, and Hawaii's first designated wildlife sanctuary, set aside in 1951. What was once a royal fishpond used to raise mullet is now a protected breeding ground for endangered native waterbirds.

The walk itself is short and easy: a paved, wheelchair-accessible path of about half a mile leads from the main gate and parking area out to a small birdwatching platform overlooking the pond. Depending on the season, visitors have a chance to spot upward of 90 recorded bird species, including the Hawaiian black-necked stilt (ae'o), the Hawaiian coot ('alae ke'oke'o), and the koloa duck — three of Hawaii's endangered endemic waterbirds.

There's no fee and no permit required; the gate is open daily from sunrise to sunset. Given its location minutes from Kahului Airport, this is a genuinely convenient stop to fill an odd half hour before a flight, or a quiet detour for anyone interested in native Hawaiian bird species without committing to a longer hike.
