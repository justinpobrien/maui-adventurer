Kealia Pond National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1992 between Kihei and Ma'alaea, protects roughly 700 acres of wetland — the largest remaining lowland wetland habitat on Maui — specifically to support two endangered native Hawaiian waterbirds found nowhere else: the Hawaiian coot and the Hawaiian stilt.

The refuge's public-facing feature is the Kealia Coastal Boardwalk, a half-mile elevated walkway that opened in 2009, giving visitors an easy, flat walk directly beside Ma'alaea Bay through the ancient wetland without disturbing the habitat. It's open every day of the year from sunrise to sunset, with a small 14-stall parking lot at the trailhead.

Birdwatching is best from August through April, when migratory species join the resident endangered waterbirds, making this a worthwhile stop for visitors interested in Hawaii's native ecology alongside its more famous beaches and reefs — a quiet, low-key counterpoint to the busier South Maui coastline nearby.
