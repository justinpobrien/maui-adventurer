Red Sand Beach, known in Hawaiian as Kaihalulu, is one of the most visually striking — and genuinely riskiest — beaches near Hana. Deep red volcanic sand fills a small cove enclosed by a crumbling cinder cliff, with a partially collapsed section forming a natural, semi-protected pool where locals sometimes swim. The color and setting are unlike anywhere else on Maui, which is exactly why it draws visitors despite the access difficulties.

Reaching it requires a half-mile hike along a narrow, unmaintained trail with steep, exposed sections and loose footing — this isn't an official, managed beach path, and it shows. Slips and falls are the leading cause of injury here, and the cove itself can produce strong rip currents, contributing to more rescues on this trail than almost anywhere else on the island.

There are no facilities, no lifeguard, and no managed parking — visitors typically park informally near Hana town and walk in through a cemetery access point. Local groups have specifically asked visitors to reconsider visiting given the safety and emergency-response challenges. Anyone who does go should wear real traction footwear, avoid the trail with kids or anyone with mobility concerns, and time the visit for morning or late afternoon to reduce both crowding and heat exposure on the exposed sections.
