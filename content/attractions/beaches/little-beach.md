Little Beach sits just over the bluff from Big Beach, separated by a short, rocky footpath around the base of Pu'u Ola'i. The setting is more intimate than its famous neighbor — a smaller cove bordered by lava outcrops on both sides — and it has built a long-standing reputation as an informal clothing-optional beach and a gathering point for Sunday sunset drum circles.

The legal reality is murkier than the custom suggests. Little Beach falls within Makena State Park, where public nudity is officially prohibited, and Hawaii's DLNR has periodically enforced that rule with citations, including a run of them after new "No Nudity" signage went up. In practice enforcement has been inconsistent, but visitors should go in aware that the beach's clothing-optional reputation isn't backed by actual legal protection.

Access and fees run through Makena State Park's general entry system — non-residents pay $5 per person plus $10 per vehicle, while Hawaii residents with valid state ID enter free. There's no lifeguard at Little Beach itself, and the swim can be rougher than it looks, so this is best suited to confident adult swimmers rather than families.
