Slaughterhouse Beach — officially Mokule'ia Bay, part of the Honolua-Mokule'ia Marine Life Conservation District — is a dramatic pocket beach reached by descending roughly 90 steps from the highway pullout above. The name and the setting both hint at a wilder character than the resort beaches just down the coast, and the ocean here lives up to it.

In summer, the bay can be calm enough for snorkeling within the protected conservation district. But this is fundamentally a winter surf and bodyboard spot: large, well-formed waves draw experienced surfers, and the same swell that makes for good rides also creates serious hazards for casual swimmers. There's no lifeguard and, notably, no warning signage at all, so the danger is easy to underestimate if you're used to the guarded beaches nearby.

Parking is limited to about 10 roadside spots that fill quickly on weekends. This isn't a beach for young kids or inexperienced swimmers in winter — it's best suited to confident surfers and bodyboarders who know how to read a shorebreak, or to anyone content to admire the cove from the overlook above.
