Honokeana Cove is one of West Maui's better-kept turtle-viewing secrets — a small, rocky cove sheltered from north swells that has become a favored resting spot for local green sea turtles, with more than a dozen sometimes visible during a single snorkeling session. The crystal-clear water here rewards an early start, since visibility drops noticeably once other visitors stir up silt from the sandy patches.

Unlike sandy-entry beaches nearby, getting into the water at Honokeana involves some rock scrambling, which naturally limits the crowd compared to easier spots like Napili Bay just up the coast. Access is public, but it's not through the Honokeana Cove Condos property itself — that's gated and restricted to residents. Instead, the common route is parking at Napili Point Resort, walking through the shared walkways, and cutting down through the shrubs to the cove.

Conditions are best in summer, when the cove is shielded from the large northwest swells that make winter visits far less reliable. There's no fee, no facilities, and no lifeguard, so this is a bring-everything, go-early kind of stop rather than a full beach day destination.
