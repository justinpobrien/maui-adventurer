Makahiku Falls is the first major waterfall encountered on the Pipiwai Trail, reached via a short, half-mile stretch from the Kipahulu Visitor Center that makes it a natural stopping point for anyone not up for the full trek to Waimoku Falls further ahead. At around 200 feet, this horsetail-style waterfall spills down cliffs Hawaiians described as "cathedral-like" — the literal meaning of Makahiku — into the valley carved by 'Ohe'o Gulch stream.

Unlike Waimoku, this is a viewing-only stop: the overlook sits above the falls rather than at its base, with no swimming access. The short trail leading to it is unpaved, often muddy, and uphill the entire way, though the modest distance (about 30 minutes each way) makes it manageable for most visitors, including families.

One real safety note applies here as much as further up the trail: this stream is prone to flash flooding after heavy rain, and the National Park Service specifically advises against hiking this section if rain is in the forecast, given how quickly conditions upstream can turn dangerous.
