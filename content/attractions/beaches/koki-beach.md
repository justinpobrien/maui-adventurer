Koki Beach is better appreciated from the sand than entered from it. This red-cinder-backed cove near Hana is scenic — dominated by the striking red hill of Ka Iwi o Pele and framed by a small offshore islet — but it's also one of the more genuinely hazardous beaches along the Road to Hana, with strong currents, powerful shorebreak, and rough seas that have caused serious injuries and drownings among visitors unfamiliar with local conditions.

There's no lifeguard, and a posted warning sign at the beach entrance reflects real risk rather than boilerplate caution. The standard local advice is blunt: don't go in unless locals are already swimming and indicate it's safe. The red cinder hill to the north adds its own hazard — the material is loose and prone to breaking off, so it's best admired from a distance rather than climbed or approached closely.

Parking is limited to a small lot just off Haneoo Road, a short distance after turning from Highway 360. Most visitors treat Koki as a scenic stop and photo opportunity on the way to Hamoa Beach just down the road, rather than a swimming destination in its own right.
