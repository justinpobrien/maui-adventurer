The Lahaina Banyan Tree, planted in 1873 and long one of Maui's most iconic living landmarks, survived the 2023 wildfire that destroyed much of the surrounding historic district — but only barely, and its recovery remains an active, closely monitored process rather than a settled fact. Arborists were forced to remove roughly 40% of the tree, including 22 trunks, in the fire's aftermath.

The encouraging news is real: clear signs of recovery were reported by Hawaii's DLNR in April 2024, with new growth visible across much of the canopy by August of that year, and that growth has continued since. Still, as of February and May 2026 reports, the tree remains in what one assessment called an "ICU" phase — hidden internal damage, including fungus discovered in a broken limb, is still being evaluated before arborists can fully certify its long-term health.

Because of this ongoing evaluation, the park itself remains closed to public entry, with only perimeter viewing available from outside the boundary. This is a meaningful change from the pre-fire experience, when visitors could walk beneath the tree's sprawling canopy — worth setting expectations accordingly for anyone planning to see it.
