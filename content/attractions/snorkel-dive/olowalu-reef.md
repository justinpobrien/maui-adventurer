Olowalu Reef anchors one of Hawaii's most ecologically significant coral systems — a 100-acre expanse recognized as a Mission Blue Hope Spot and considered a "mother reef" that seeds coral growth for surrounding islands including Lana'i and Moloka'i. Locally nicknamed Turtle Reef, it hosts multiple turtle cleaning stations where green sea turtles gather to have algae picked off their shells by reef fish, sometimes with a dozen turtles visible at once.

Beyond turtles, Olowalu shelters an unusually large manta ray population — reportedly over 350 individuals, making it one of the largest manta habitats anywhere — plus abundant reef fish and migrating humpback whales visible offshore in winter. Its shelter from trade winds sets it apart from more exposed West Maui snorkel spots, keeping conditions calmer more consistently throughout the day.

Access is informal: find the reef around mile marker 14 on Honoapi'ilani Highway and park along the roadside rather than in a dedicated lot. There's no lifeguard and minimal facilities, making this more of a focused snorkeling stop than an all-day beach outing.
