The Plum Trail is the gentlest leg of the Polipoli trail network, a wide, relatively flat path through mixed forest that gives hikers a break after the steeper Redwood Trail descent nearby. Its name comes from the plum trees scattered along the route — a legacy of early reforestation plantings — which produce fruit hikers can sample in season.

On its own, the Plum Trail is a short, easy connector rather than a destination hike, but it's typically combined with the Redwood, Polipoli, and Haleakala Ridge Trails into a fuller loop covering roughly 3.5 to 4.7 miles total, with moderate overall difficulty driven more by the connecting trails' elevation change than by the Plum Trail segment itself.

As with the rest of the Polipoli network, reaching the trailhead requires navigating the rough final stretch of Waipoli Road, where 4WD is effectively required and 2WD vehicles risk getting stuck or being fined past the posted point. Given some uncertainty around the current status of neighboring trails like Redwood, it's worth checking recent trip reports before planning a full loop hike here.
