Waianapanapa State Park's black sand beach is one of the most photographed stops on the Road to Hana, and one of the most tightly managed — a striking cove of jet-black volcanic sand framed by lava rock formations, sea arches, and blowholes, with dense green cliffs rising behind. Beyond the beach itself, the park includes coastal hiking trails, lava tube caves, and a heiau (ancient Hawaiian temple site), making it worth more than a quick photo stop if time allows.

Access now runs entirely through a reservation system: non-resident visitors must book a timed entry slot online, up to 30 days in advance, with no same-day bookings and no walk-ins permitted without one. Fees run $10 per vehicle for parking plus $5 per person for entry (Hawaii residents are exempt from both), and each reservation covers a window of roughly 2.5 to 3 hours to help spread out crowds.

The swimming here is scenic more than practical — surf and currents can be strong, and the rocky, uneven entry isn't especially forgiving. Most visitors come for the sand, the sea caves, and the short coastal trail rather than a long ocean swim, and it's worth building the reservation requirement into any Road to Hana itinerary well before the drive.
