Kanaha Beach Park, tucked beside the Kahului Airport, is Maui's premier launch point for windsurfing and kitesurfing, and its 88 acres are essentially built around the sport. Steady trade winds — strongest from about May through October — funnel between Haleakala and the West Maui Mountains to create some of the most reliable wind conditions in the world for these sports.

The park splits into distinct zones: Naska Beach on the west side is the beginner-friendly launch where most instructors run lessons, while Kite Beach (also called Teach Beach) to the east draws more experienced riders. Lifeguards patrol on jetskis and enforce basic time-and-place rules — kitesurfing is generally allowed from 11am to 8pm within the park's 6am-8pm gate hours, keeping mornings calmer for other beachgoers.

Beyond the water sports, Kanaha has the full slate of county facilities: parking, restrooms, showers, picnic tables, and a volleyball court, making it a reasonable stop even for visitors who have no intention of getting on a board — the sheer number of kites in the air is a spectacle on its own.
