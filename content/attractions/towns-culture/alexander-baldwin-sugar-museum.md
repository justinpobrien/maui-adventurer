The Alexander & Baldwin Sugar Museum documents two centuries of Maui's sugar plantation history, housed near the site of the former Pu'unene Mill — a defining industry for the island's economy, demographics, and culture until the plantation's closure roughly a decade ago. The museum remains open and operational as of 2026, though its future is tied to broader redevelopment plans for the surrounding mill site, which are still in the works as of early 2026.

Access requires more advance planning than most museums: hours run Monday through Thursday only, 10am to 2pm, with the museum closed entirely Friday through Sunday. Reservations are currently required and should be made online ahead of a visit rather than assuming walk-in access.

Admission runs $7 for adults, $5 for seniors, $2 for children 6 to 12, and is free for kids five and under — modest pricing for a museum covering a genuinely significant piece of Hawaii's economic and immigration history, given how central the sugar industry was to bringing Japanese, Chinese, Portuguese, Filipino, and Korean workers to Maui.
