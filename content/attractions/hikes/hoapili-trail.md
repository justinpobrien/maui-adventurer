The Hoapili Trail, also known as the King's Highway (Ala Loa), follows a centuries-old stone path built to circle the entire island of Maui, and this remaining stretch runs along the driest, most remote part of the south coast beyond La Perouse Bay. At 5.5 miles and roughly 6 hours round trip, it's rated difficult — not for elevation gain, but for the sheer harshness of the terrain and climate it crosses.

The trail traverses fields of jagged 'a'a lava, sharp enough to shred anything less than sturdy hiking boots, through a landscape so dry and barren it's nearly devoid of vegetation for long stretches. There's no shade anywhere along the route and no reliable water source, making this one of the more physically demanding hikes on Maui despite its relatively modest distance and flat profile.

The historical stonework itself — sections of the original ali'i-era paved road, built to connect coastal villages around the island — is the main draw for hikers willing to take on the conditions, along with genuinely remote coastal views toward Kaho'olawe. The trailhead sits at the La Perouse Bay parking lot, the literal end of the paved road in south Maui, making this a fitting last stop before turning back toward civilization.
