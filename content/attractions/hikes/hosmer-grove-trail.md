Hosmer Grove is a short, easy loop near the entrance to Haleakala National Park, and its unusual landscape makes it worth the brief stop even for visitors mainly headed to the summit. The grove itself is the legacy of an early-20th-century forestry experiment: a mix of non-native conifers planted here decades ago now stands beside native Hawaiian shrubland, creating a genuinely odd juxtaposition of ecosystems within a few hundred yards of trail.

At roughly 0.5 miles with minimal elevation change, this is one of the easiest walks in the park, making it a sensible first stop to help adjust to elevation before continuing up toward the 10,000-foot summit area. It's also considered one of the better birdwatching spots in Haleakala, with a reasonable chance of spotting native Hawaiian forest birds alongside more common species.

Entry falls under the standard Haleakala National Park fee, currently around $25 to $30 per vehicle depending on recent adjustments, valid for 7 days and covering the whole park. No separate permit is needed for this trail specifically.
