Halemau'u Trail offers the fastest way down into Haleakala's crater, dropping about 1,390 feet over 2.8 miles of tightly engineered stone switchbacks before reaching the crater floor at 3.7 miles total. It's rated difficult, and steep enough that the trail's narrow ridgeline approach — nicknamed Rainbow Bridge for the sweeping views it offers into the crater below — is as memorable as the switchbacks themselves.

Because it's the steepest of Haleakala's main access trails, Halemau'u trades length for grade: hikers looking for the shortest path in and out of the crater will find it here, at the cost of a genuinely demanding descent and, more importantly, an equally demanding climb back out at high altitude.

A common way to experience both this trail and Sliding Sands without doubling back on the same route is to hike one direction on each, arranging a shuttle or timed pickup between the two trailheads — a more ambitious full-day itinerary than either trail alone, but one that avoids repeating scenery. As with all crater trails, the standard Haleakala National Park entrance fee covers access, with no separate permit needed for a day hike.
