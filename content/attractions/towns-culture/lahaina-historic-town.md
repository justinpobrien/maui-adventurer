Lahaina, once Maui's most-visited historic town and former capital of the Hawaiian Kingdom, was devastated by the August 2023 wildfire that destroyed much of its historic core. Recovery has been slow but genuinely underway, and a significant milestone lands right around now: Front Street between Papalaua and Shaw Streets reopens to regular vehicle traffic on August 1, 2026, along with Dickenson and Prison Streets, marking the first time in three years that cars can drive through this stretch without restricted hours.

This is an important but partial recovery. Some businesses — including a returning Kimo's restaurant and the 'Ulu o Lele Marketplace — are moving forward as part of this reopening, and more than a dozen businesses are part of the renewed commercial activity. But the blocks of Front Street most associated with pre-fire Lahaina — the art galleries, harbor-view restaurants, and historic storefronts — remain behind construction fencing, and streets like Market Street and Papelekane Street stay closed for ongoing infrastructure work, including the state's dredging of Lahaina Harbor.

Visitors planning a Lahaina stop in 2026 should treat this as a town in active, meaningful recovery rather than a fully restored historic district — worth visiting to witness and support the rebuilding, but not yet the pre-2023 experience.
